### Design improvements

* **Replace the Western lab photo:** Instead of a close‑up microscope image, use an illustration or photograph that feels harmonious with traditional medicine.  Possible options:

  * A softly lit still‑life featuring herbs, dried roots, and a mortar and pestle alongside a modern lab vial — symbolising the bridge between TCM and clinical testing.
  * A watercolour illustration of a ginkgo or lotus leaf with subtle geometric overlays hinting at data and diagnostics.
  * A calm landscape with misty mountains and a superimposed diagram of meridians or yin–yang, reinforcing the integration of ancient wisdom with modern science.

  These alternatives preserve the notion of “complete picture” while avoiding the cold, high‑tech look of a microscope.

* **Refine typography hierarchy:** The heading sizes and spacing are working well, but you can increase the line height on subheadings (like the text under the hero) for even better readability.  Ensure body copy stays in Plus Jakarta Sans at a comfortable size (16–18 px) and keep headings in Lora with generous letter spacing to evoke classical texts.

* **Enhance navigation feedback:** Consider adding a subtle underline or colour change to the active nav item (“Home”, “How it Works”, etc.) so users can tell where they are.  You could use a jade‑coloured underline that animates into view on hover.

* **Consistent button styles:** Your primary button (deep jade with rounded corners) looks cohesive; ensure secondary buttons (like “Learn How It Works”) consistently use the cream background with jade outline and a hover state that slightly darkens the outline.

### Image suggestions for the “How It Works” flow

To illustrate the four‑step journey without undermining the warm, holistic feel:

1. **Step 1 – Schedule tests:** Use an image showing both a lab kit and TCM diagnostics.  This could be a stylised illustration with test tubes and a pulse‑reading wrist, or a scene where a practitioner examines a patient’s tongue with herbal imagery in the background.

2. **Step 2 – Get insights:** Choose an illustration of a practitioner (silhouette or minimalist drawing) reviewing a patient chart that combines Western lab graphs with TCM meridian maps.  Alternatively, an image of a serene consultation room with scrolls and a laptop open to lab results can visually convey the blend of ancient and modern insights.

3. **Step 3 – Take action:** Depict a person engaging in a holistic health practice, such as sipping a cup of herbal tea, preparing a powdered formula, practising Qi Gong or having an acupuncture session.  This conveys the personalised protocols that come out of the analysis.

4. **Step 4 – Monitor for life:** Use an image that represents continuity and growth, such as a tree going through the four seasons, a circle of the five elements, or a hand holding a smartphone displaying health metrics in jade and earth tones.  This reinforces the idea of ongoing care and pattern recognition without feeling clinical.

When sourcing images, stick to a cohesive colour palette (deep greens, creams, muted earth tones) and consider illustrations over stock photos to maintain a warm, timeless quality.  Subtle textures or painted edges can help tie them into the paper‑like backgrounds used on the site.

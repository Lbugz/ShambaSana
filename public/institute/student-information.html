The required courses are virtual and taken in sequence. Complete Course I before beginning Course II, and complete both before beginning Course III.

Course I — Foundations of Chinese Medicine · Fall 2026

Underway · Registration closed. No previous Chinese medicine study is required for this introductory course.

Explore Chinese medical thought, Daoist perspectives, Yin–Yang, Five Elements, Three Treasures, Qi, Blood, body fluids, Jing, Shen, Zang-Fu organ theory and introductory channel concepts.

Learning outcome: Explain central theories and describe relationships between organs, elements and vital substances using introductory educational examples.

Course II — The Psyche in Chinese Medicine · Spring 2027

Registration open · Course I required before starting.

Study Shen, Hun, Po, Yi and Zhi; the Seven Emotions; emotional-Qi and organ relationships; sleep and emotional experiences; and introductory psycho-emotional case interpretation.

Learning outcome: Compare traditional frameworks of mind and emotion, explain their relationships and recognize when further qualified assessment is appropriate. Traditional patterns are not presented as psychiatric diagnoses.

Core reading: Giovanni Maciocia, The Psyche in Chinese Medicine. The required edition and final reading list will be confirmed before class.

Course III — Advanced Diagnosis & Treatment · Summer 2027

Registration open · Courses I and II required before starting.

Explore the Eight Principles, Qi–Blood–Fluids, organ patterns, Five Elements, channels, Six Stages, Four Levels, Three Burners and introductory tongue and pulse concepts.

Learning outcome: Organize fictional case information, distinguish traditional patterns, explain conceptual treatment strategies and identify situations requiring qualified assessment.

Case exercises are educational. This virtual course does not include independent patient treatment or practical workshops.

Spring and Summer 2027 dates, session times, time zones and instructional hours will be confirmed before payment. Future Foundations intake dates have not been announced.

Preparation & admission.

We welcome adult learners from diverse educational, professional and cultural backgrounds who are willing to complete readings and assessments, participate thoughtfully and respect the learning community.

You may register for a later course while progressing through the earlier courses. Tell us whether you have completed, are studying or have registered for each prerequisite. The Institute reviews this information against its records; required courses must be completed before the next course starts.

  1. Select your course and term in the separate registration form.
  2. Provide your contact details, prerequisite status and earlier cohort information.
  3. The Institute reviews your preparation and confirms any outstanding steps.
  4. Review the confirmed schedule, final handbook, financial policies and enrollment agreement.
  5. Complete the agreed tuition payment after acceptance and review.
  6. Receive enrollment confirmation, orientation information and class access once the required steps are complete.

Registration is not automatic admission. Payment does not replace prerequisite review or completion.

Virtual learning

Prepare for online classes with a reliable internet connection, a suitable device and access to the course materials. Live discussion, lesson presentations, reading and educational case examples support learning. Recording availability and access periods will be confirmed for each cohort.

Assessment

Learning may be assessed through quizzes, written examinations, reading assignments, participation, presentations and case analysis. The syllabus will specify assessment methods, grading thresholds and deadlines.

Attendance

Regular attendance and preparation matter. Attend on time, complete assigned reading and participate respectfully. Minimum attendance and any make-up arrangements will be stated before enrollment.

Support & access

Raise learning difficulties or accommodation needs with the Institute before your course begins where possible. The support contact and request process will be included in the final student information supplied before enrollment.

A pathway to completion.

The program’s completion pathway requires all three core courses, passing grades, required academic assessments, attendance and applicable administrative requirements. Exact thresholds and the final certificate title will be confirmed in the approved handbook.

A completion certificate records educational achievement. This program does not itself confer professional licensure or authority to diagnose, prescribe, perform acupuncture or provide independent clinical care. Electives and workshops do not replace the required core courses.

A respectful learning community.

  • Treat classmates and instructors with dignity and respect across cultural, religious and philosophical differences.
  • Submit original work, acknowledge sources and follow the instructor’s guidance on AI-assisted assignments.
  • Protect confidentiality. Use fictional or appropriately anonymized cases and avoid sharing identifiable health information in class or registration messages.
  • Ask permission before recording or sharing classes and learning materials.
  • Recognize the boundaries of educational case studies and your level of preparation.

Discuss routine learning concerns with your instructor where appropriate. Serious safety, harassment or misconduct concerns should be raised directly with administration. The final handbook will provide the contact route, complaint review process and any disciplinary procedures.
